Sushi Robata sits in a strip mall on Frankford Road in Far North Dallas — easy to park, easy to miss if you don't know it's there. The interior is dark and unpretentious: brown booths, traditional Japanese noren curtains hanging at the sushi bar, maneki-neko fabric on the walls, a fish case running the length of the counter. It's the kind of place where the room is secondary to what's in front of the chef.

The real reason to come is the daily sashimi and sushi specials list, which is one of the most extensive we've seen at a Dallas Japanese restaurant. The rotating selection covers fish sourced specifically by region: bluefin tuna from Malta, saury from Hokkaido, barracuda from Kanagawa, king salmon from New Zealand, fresh sea scallops from Miyagi, blackfin seabass from Kagoshima, green sea urchin from Hokkaido, purple sea urchin from California, flounder with marinated kelp from Korea — the list runs long and changes with availability. For a sashimi-first approach to a meal, it's a genuinely strong option in Dallas.

Two specific highlights: the Japanese amberjack nigiri — clean, buttery, the kind of fish that makes the case for ordering from the specials rather than the standard menu — and the real wasabi, freshly grated and served alongside. The difference between real wasabi and the green paste that passes for it everywhere else is not subtle. If a restaurant is serving the real thing, that tells you something about how seriously they take the raw bar.

One note on the specialty rolls: many of them are built around imitation crab, which is a non-starter for us and worth knowing before you order. If that's not a concern, the roll selection is broad. If it is, stick to nigiri and sashimi — that's where this kitchen earns its reputation anyway.

Service was sufficient. Not memorable in either direction — the table was handled competently and the specials were explained when asked.

The price point is fair for the quality of fish on the specials list. The assorted sashimi or sushi special (from $45) is the efficient way to sample the kitchen's range without navigating the full menu. Individual nigiri from the specials run $9–$20 per piece, with sashimi portions priced accordingly. It's not cheap, but it's priced honestly for sourced fish with origin detail on the menu.

**The verdict:** Sushi Robata is a low-key North Dallas sushi spot with a daily specials list that punches well above the strip mall setting. Go for the sashimi, skip the specialty rolls if imitation crab isn't your thing, and don't expect the room to do any of the work. The fish does.
