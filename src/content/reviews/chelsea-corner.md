Chelsea Corner has been on Cedar Springs since 1974, which in Dallas bar years is basically geological time. The awning, the room, the long bar stacked floor-to-ceiling with bottles — it all carries that lived-in credibility you can't manufacture. This is a real neighborhood bar, and it knows exactly what it is.

The bar program is the starting point. The margarita comes in a blue-rimmed glass and is built correctly — not overly sweet, properly balanced, the kind you order again without thinking. The Old Fashioned is well-made: orange peel, proper dilution, good base spirit. The bartenders know the room and the speed is there even when it's busy, which on weekends it consistently is.

The food is bar food done right, which is its own category of good. The smash burger is the move: double smashed patties, melted cheese, properly seared crust, wrapped in Chelsea Corner paper and served with a pile of skinny seasoned fries. It's the kind of burger you eat in four bites because you can't stop. The fries hold up on their own — thin, seasoned, not an afterthought.

The boneless buffalo wings are the right appetizer for this room: sauced, hot, served with ranch, celery, and carrot on the Chelsea Corner platter. Not reinventing anything, just executing the format well. The buffalo chicken wrap is a solid lunch option — fried chicken, lettuce, flour tortilla, dipping sauce — unpretentious and filling.

The atmosphere does a lot of work here. The bar is long and social, the TVs catch whatever game is on, and the crowd is the Oak Lawn neighborhood mix that's been coming here for decades alongside newer regulars who found it and didn't leave. It earns the loyalty.

**The verdict:** Chelsea Corner is the standard Dallas neighborhood bar against which other neighborhood bars get measured. Go for the smash burger and a margarita, stay as long as the game runs.
