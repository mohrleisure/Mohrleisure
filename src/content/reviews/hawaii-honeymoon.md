Eleven days across two islands. We started in Maui (five nights between Kaanapali and Wailea), crossed to O'ahu for the North Shore and then Waikiki, and flew home sunburned with a very strong opinion about mai tais. What follows is the complete honest record — what to book, what to skip, what surprised us.

---

## Where We Stayed

### [Westin Kaanapali](https://www.marriott.com/en-us/hotels/oggws-the-westin-kaanapali-ocean-resort-villas/overview) — Maui (4 nights)

This was the right call for a Maui base. Walking distance to Black Rock for snorkeling, connected via a beachside path to neighboring resorts, restaurants, and shops. Multiple pools, multiple bars, an arcade, coffee, and shops on property. They keep parrots and flamingos on site, which is either charming or unexpected depending on your mood when you encounter them. We had a daily credit that covered coffee, breakfast, and snacks — put that to use immediately. Location and amenities are the story here; the rooms do everything you need.

### [Grand Wailea](https://www.grandwailea.com) — Maui (1 night)

One night here because the rate put more nights out of reach — and honestly, we stretched our arrival early and pushed departure late to squeeze out the full amenity experience. Worth doing exactly that way. The property is enormous and over-the-top in the best sense: pools built like a waterslide system, multiple restaurants and bars, a spa. At their market, **Loulu**, get the coconut soft serve sundae. It's $20. We went twice in the same day and then bought all the ingredients to recreate it at home. We regret nothing.

Also on property: **[Humuhumunukunukuapua'a](https://www.grandwailea.com)** (named after the Hawaiian state fish), a bar built around a fish tank. Strong mai tai — third-best we had in Hawaii, for those keeping score.

### [Marriott North Shore](https://www.marriott.com/en-us/hotels/hnloa-courtyard-oahu-north-shore/overview) — O'ahu (2 nights)

Practical and well-priced for a North Shore base. The Ritz Turtle Bay is further north, which would have added drive time to our activities further south — so the Marriott made more logistical sense. Rooms are updated and the location works. This isn't a destination property; it's a good base for doing things.

### [Hilton Hawaiian Village](https://hiltonhawaiianvillage.com) — O'ahu (1 night, Waikiki)

The property does a five-minute fireworks show on Fridays, which draws a crowd. We stayed in the Rainbow Tower — rooms are nice, but the elevator situation is genuinely bad. They use a lobby-keypad system similar to NYC residential buildings, and it does not work the way it should. Expect 10–15 minute waits to get to your floor. Check in before 3pm; the afternoon rush means a long wait at the desk.

## Maui

### [Miso Phat](https://misophat.com) — Night 1 Dinner

A solid sushi start to the trip. We ordered the **tuna and amberjack nigiri** and the **TNT roll**. On the rolls, you can substitute real crab for the imitation crab for $4 — do it. Friendly service, good food. Parking is a known headache; we circled once and got lucky, but build in the time.

### Molokini Crater — Day 2

We went with **[Red Line Rafting](https://redlinerafting.com)** and chose their 2-hour tour over the longer option. The longer tour adds a second snorkel site, but it also adds more time on a very fast, very bouncy raft — and we're both susceptible to seasickness. The ride out takes about 15 minutes, and by the time we turned back on the short tour, we were already starting to feel it. Good call.

The snorkeling itself is genuinely unlike anywhere else we've been. The crater wall drops sharply and the variety of fish along it is remarkable. Two hours was enough — most of that time is in the water and we saw everything without feeling rushed.

### [Merriman's](https://www.merrimanshawaii.com) — Night 2 Dinner

The best meal we had in Hawaii, full stop. We arrived right at opening and got seated without a reservation — for a party larger than two, book ahead.

They make their mai tai in-house with real ingredients and are visibly proud of it. We tried every mai tai we could across the whole trip, and Merriman's was first place without debate. The **sashimi comes with tableside-ground wasabi**, which is a different experience than the pre-mixed version. We also ordered the **macadamia nut crusted fresh catch**, the special of the day (fresh caught fish, prawns, and a mini veggie pot pie), and their **coconut cake** for dessert.

The setting is a peninsula overlooking a bay. During our dinner, we had a front-row view of a traditional Hawaiian wedding on the beach below.

### [Sea Maui Surf](https://seamauisurf.com) — Day 3

Beginners lesson with a guide named Tyler. He had us both standing on the first attempt. Their location is convenient to the Westin and other Kaanapali resorts. We don't normally buy the photo packages they push after activities — we made an exception here. The camera quality was genuinely impressive. Private lessons ran around $300 total; photos were around $90. Worth it.

### Honolua Bay — Day 3 Snorkel

The walk down through the jungle to reach the bay is a genuinely cool experience. The snorkeling itself was a different story — rocky entry, murky water. We know conditions vary day to day and weather is a real factor here, so check before you go. We'd try it again under better conditions, but it wasn't the highlight we'd hoped for.

### [Monkeypod](https://www.monkeypodkitchen.com) — Night 3 Dinner

Owned by the same group as Merriman's, and the mai tai has a strong reputation. We'd put it second in our ranking — very good, but it didn't beat the original. Happy hour brought the **pork and jalapeño pizza** down to a reasonable price, and we ordered **ahi tacos** alongside it. The **banana cake** was the standout — genuinely memorable. Lively spot, worth the visit.

### [Maui Gold Pineapple Farm](https://mauipineappletour.com) — Day 4

A guided tour through how pineapples are grown, harvested, and used on the island. The history is interesting, the guide was engaging, and the tour includes a pineapple to take home. A note on logistics: we were going to be in Hawaii for several more days and didn't want to haul a ripening pineapple around, so the Westin cut it for us. You're also allowed to fly with them as a carry-on, which is notable since most fresh produce can't leave Hawaii.

### Twin Falls — Day 4 Hike

A short, accessible hike with waterfalls and dense greenery. Parking is easy and cheap. This is a 30-to-45 minute stop that you can fold in around other activities without much planning. Worth the detour.

### [Mama's Fish House](https://mamasfishhouse.com) — Night 4 Dinner

We booked this months out, which is what you have to do. The setting is striking — a converted beach house with the ocean right there — and the menu is entirely built around what was caught that day, by named fishermen listed on the menu. Every dish is designed around a specific catch, which makes it feel different from other "fresh fish" restaurants that use that as a generic claim. If you're watching the budget, skip the drinks and focus on the food — that's where the money is well spent.

### [Paia Fish Market](https://paiafishmarket.com) — Day 5

After the Grand Wailea, we drove about 15 minutes to Paia Fish Market for dinner. The price difference versus Grand Wailea dining is significant. The line was long. It moved faster than it looked. Simple menu, generous portions, well-executed — the kind of place that does one thing and does it right.

## O'ahu

### [Haleiwa Joe's](https://www.haleiwajoes.com) — Night 6 Dinner

Two locations; the Kaneohe location is the one to go to. The views are the first thing — the dining room looks out over a dramatic valley — and the food backs it up. No reservations taken, so we waited about an hour for our table. You can grab a bar seat while you wait and order from the menu, which is what we did. Recommend.

### [Kualoa Ranch](https://www.kualoa.com) — Day 7

The highlight of O'ahu. Dozens of films and television shows have been filmed here — Jurassic Park, Lost, Godzilla, Jumanji, among others — and the guided tour covers that history while driving through the working ranch and film locations. You can choose between a guided UTV tour or a self-drive UTV; we took the guided version, which costs the same but includes the history and context. Worth it for the difference in experience.

### Lanikai Pillbox Hike — Day 7

Moderate to challenging, with panoramic views of O'ahu's windward coast and the Mokulua Islands. The views justify the effort. Parking is the problem — it shares a lot with Lanikai Beach, which is itself extremely popular, and we circled for 30 minutes before finding a spot. Once parked, we did the hike, changed at the car, and walked to the beach. Do both. Parking is genuinely difficult — go early, consider sunrise timing.

### [Kbaybro's Poke](https://kbaybros.com) — Night 7 Dinner

Recommended by our Kualoa guide. Sweet staff, good selection, better-priced than Foodland's fresh poke counter. We got it to go and ate it for sunset at Sunset Beach. Perfect.

### [Royal Hawaiian Golf Club](https://royalhawaiiangc.com) — Day 8

Our surf guide in Maui had recommended this, and the setting delivers: the front nine genuinely evokes Jurassic Park — dense jungle, dramatic elevation changes. The course is very difficult. They have a saying that whatever your handicap is, that's how many balls you'll lose. The area gets significant rainfall, so expect cart path only and wet conditions that slow down play. About $200 for 18 holes, $80 for club rentals.

### [Ritz-Carlton Turtle Bay](https://www.ritzcarlton.com/en/hotels/hnlkz-the-ritz-carlton-oahu-turtle-bay) — Night 8 Drinks

We went for drinks rather than dinner. The lobby bar overlooks the water with a fireplace — a nice space. Creative craft cocktails, friendly staff. Good way to end the North Shore portion of the trip.

### Forty Niner — Day 9 Breakfast

A breakfast stop as we made our way south toward Waikiki. Worth knowing for a morning meal before hitting the road.

### [Maui Brewing Co](https://www.mbcrestaurants.com) — Night 9 Dinner

We couldn't get a walk-in at the higher-profile spots on the Waikiki strip that night, but Maui Brewing Co took us. The handmade pizza was a genuine surprise — a 10 out of 10, and we were still thinking about it while writing this. A useful back-pocket option for nights when reservations fall through.

### [Hanauma Bay](https://web5.hnl.info/pros-ticketing/event/hbay-online-reservation) — Day 10

You need a reservation, made two days in advance. The site opens at roughly 7am for reservations. Parking costs extra. Note: we visited with a storm system approaching, and conditions showed it — shallow, murky water, reduced visibility. We brushed against coral and cut our toe, which was the cue to call it. Under better conditions, this would be a very different snorkel. Check before you go.

### Kalapawai Market — Day 10 Lunch

A convenient stop on the way. Easy and quick — nothing that'll make you change your plans around it, but a solid option if you need a bite between activities.

### [Strip Steak](https://www.stripsteak.com) — Night 10 Dinner

After multiple days of fish, we were ready for a steak. Strip Steak delivered. The happy hour is genuinely good — we worked through cocktails and ordered from the happy hour menu, then pushed into dinner with the **bone marrow burger**, a **ribeye**, and a **chocolate dessert**. This restaurant consistently surprised us. Walk-ins accepted. If you want a high-quality dinner in Waikiki without competing for a reservation, this is it.

### [Diamond Head Crater](https://gostateparks.hawaii.gov) — Day 11

A reservation is required here too. We arrived about 30 minutes early and were fine. The hike is mostly paved, about an hour round trip, and gets steep in sections. The views from the summit over Waikiki and the coastline are worth it. We did this the morning of our flight back to Maui for the connection home, and the timing worked cleanly.

## The Mai Tai Rankings

We tried every mai tai we encountered, and the order was not close:

1. **[Merriman's](https://www.merrimanshawaii.com)** — real ingredients, made with care, the benchmark
2. **[Monkeypod](https://www.monkeypodkitchen.com)** — very good, owns a strong local reputation for a reason
3. **[Humuhumunukunukuapua'a](https://www.grandwailea.com) at Grand Wailea** — good, right for the setting

---

## A Few Practical Notes

- **Inter-island flights:** We used Southwest for both inter-island legs. Cheap, easy, no issues.
- **Molokini seasickness:** If you're prone, take the shorter tour and sit toward the front on the ride out.
- **Honolua Bay:** Check surf and water conditions before committing — it's worth it on the right day.
- **Grand Wailea:** Arrive early and stay late on your checkout day; the amenities reward it.
- **[Hanauma Bay](https://web5.hnl.info/pros-ticketing/event/hbay-online-reservation):** Book exactly two days out, early in the morning, or you'll miss the reservation window.
- **Lanikai Pillbox / Beach parking:** Budget extra time and aim for early morning.

Hawaii is now a permanent fixture in our travel plans. It's not a once-and-done trip. Every island we didn't make it to this time is already on the list.
